## Commmemorating 85 Years: A Legacy of Resilience

Join us as we mark the 85th Anniversary of [Executive Order 9066](https://encyclopedia.densho.org/Executive_Order_9066/) and honor the
Japanese and Japanese Americans who were forcibly removed from their homes
and unjustly incarcerated during World War II, including the over 7,500 held at
the Puyallup Assembly Center on the site of what was then the Puyallup
Fairgrounds.

**Their stories must never be forgotten.**
 
**Their history must never be erased.**

## Speakers

#### Frank Abe

<Image src={frankAbe} alt="Frank Abe, photo by Kayla Isomura" style="float:left; max-width:35%; padding: .5em .5em .5em 0;" />The Day of Remembrance was created in 1978. Frank Abe along with Shosuke Sasaki, Henry Miyatake, Chuck Kato, Mike Nakata and Ken Nakano organized the nation's first Day of Remembrance.

Frank is the lead author of the graphic novel, [WE HEREBY REFUSE: Japanese American Resistance to Wartime Incarceration](https://resisters.com/we-hereby-refuse/), 
and co-editor of the Penguin Classics anthology of [THE LITERATURE OF JAPANESE AMERICAN INCARCERATION](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/570029/the-literature-of-japanese-american-incarceration-by-edited-with-an-introduction-by-frank-abe-and-floyd-cheung/). 
He wrote and directed the award-winning PBS documentary [CONSCIENCE AND THE CONSTITUTION](https://www.resisters.com/conscience/the_film/the_filmmakers/index.html),” 
and is currently developing a new stage adaptation of [John Okada’s NO-NO BOY](https://uwapress.uw.edu/book/9780295994048/no-no-boy/). He blogs at [Resisters.com](https://resisters.com). 

 
#### Anna Hasegawa

<Image src={annaHasegawa} alt="Anna Hasegawa" style="float:left; max-width:35%; padding: .5em .5em .5em 0;" />Anna Sakaizawa Hasegawa was born in 1927, the middle child of Fsao and Reiko Sakaizawa, first generation Japanese immigrants. In 1941, Anna was a freshman at Polytech High School in Long Beach, California.  

Anna will share the story of her father’s arrest by the FBI and the landlord family who stored their family’s belongings when they were sent to Santa Anita Assembly Center, then to Jerome, Arkansas, and to Amache, Colorado. Anna received a scholarship and was able to leave camp to attend Maryville College in Tennessee where she received her degree in psychology and education. Anna’s students ranged from pre-school to university, and she finished her career as a special education teacher in Illinois. Anna currently lives in Des Moines, Washington.
